## Part 1: AI + Automations Delivery Lead

### A. Talking Points (reference during the conversation)

**Open with the frame — the work already exists:**
"We're already doing AI implementations and building Rovo Agent governance. That work is happening ad hoc — no single owner, no delivery structure, no consistent process. The role doesn't create new work. It formalizes and accelerates what's already happening."

**Make the cost of waiting concrete:**
- Without the role: AI initiatives fragment across the team, governance is reactive, delivery quality varies by whoever picks it up, no one owns outcomes
- With the role: one owner, consistent delivery process, governance built proactively, measurable results the department can report on
- "The question isn't whether we need someone owning this — we already do the work. It's how long we keep doing it informally before it becomes a problem."

**Reference the AI momentum (likely happened while you were on sabbatical):**
- Note specific AI initiatives or conversations that moved forward while you were away
- "There's clearly momentum here. The role makes sure that momentum turns into delivered results, not just experiments."

**Position comp as role market value, not personal ask:**
- "I looked at market data for AI delivery leads and AI-adjacent lead roles in Utah. The range I'm seeing is $120K–$130K base for this scope. I want to make sure we price the role competitively and in a way that reflects what we're asking it to do."
- Close with a question, not a demand: "Does that align with the band you're building for this role?"

**Anticipated pushback:**

| They say | You say |

| "We don't have the budget right now" | "What would need to happen for the budget to open up? I'd rather know the path than guess at it." |

| "We need to see more results first" | "What specific results? I want to know exactly what success looks like so we're measuring the same thing." |

**Your walk-away floor:** $115K base. Below that, you're taking lead responsibility at senior IC comp. Don't say this number out loud — it's your private line.

## Part 2: MX IDE — Independent PM Project

This is a separate track from the delivery lead role. This is Mitchell pursuing AI PM work independently.

### The problem nobody is solving

FI/Fintech clients in MX's pipeline slow down at the implementation stage. They have to build out and connect the implementation with or without MX delivery team support. Whether they get help or do it themselves, the build-and-connect process is the bottleneck. This is a problem Mitchell keeps hearing about — and nobody is solving for it.

### The vision

An IDE-like workspace where FI/Fintech clients can accelerate their own implementation of MX capabilities. Not replacing the delivery team — giving clients tooling that makes the build-and-connect process faster, clearer, and less dependent on back-and-forth with MX internal teams.

Evolved from the Prototype Composer capstone. Same core problem (implementation speed), shifted from prospect demos to active client implementation support.

### Why this is PM work

This project hits every PM competency the delivery lead role doesn't:
- **Discovery:** What specifically slows clients down in the implementation? Where do they get stuck? What do they wish they had?
- **Problem definition:** Scoping the exact gap between what clients need and what MX currently provides
- **User research:** Talking to clients, SAs, delivery teams — triangulating the real bottleneck
- **Product scoping:** What does v1 look like? What's the smallest thing that proves the concept?
- **Success metrics:** Implementation time reduction, client satisfaction, pipeline velocity

### How this fits the bigger picture

- Delivery lead role proves Mitchell can own and ship AI initiatives
- MX IDE proves Mitchell can decide WHAT to build and WHY
- Together: a complete AI PM portfolio — delivery + product strategy
- This lives on LinkedIn and on Mitchell's resume regardless of what MX does with the delivery lead role
