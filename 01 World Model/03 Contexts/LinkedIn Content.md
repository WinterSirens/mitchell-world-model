## What this file is
The operating guide for LinkedIn content decisions. Use this when evaluating content ideas, deciding what to post next, or checking whether a draft is on-strategy.

**The goal:** Convert LinkedIn readers into newsletter subscribers by hitting the exact pain points of the Stuck Implementer. See `Stuck Implementer.md` for the audience reference.

---

## The Core Thesis
The Stuck Implementer is already on LinkedIn. They're scrolling past "10x productivity" posts and feeling behind. Content that **names their specific frustration** — not AI in general, but their experience — is what stops the scroll.

> When you name the pain, they feel seen. That's the moment they click follow and eventually subscribe.

---

## Five Content Pillars

### Pillar 1 — Name the Frustration (Empathy Posts)
**Goal:** Stop the scroll. Make them feel seen. Build trust before any pitch.
**Format:** Short, 3-6 lines. No advice. Just: "You've felt this, right?"
**Examples:** "You didn't fail at AI. The workflow was just designed for someone else." / "70% of managers are experimenting with AI. Fewer than 10% are optimistic. That gap has a name."

### Pillar 2 — Reframe the Problem (Perspective Shifts)
**Goal:** Show there's a solvable root cause. Plant: systems > tools.
**Format:** 5-8 lines, contrarian stat or twist. End with an implicit question.
**Examples:** "The problem isn't your prompts. It's that you haven't defined what 'done' looks like for AI." / "Generic input = generic output. The tool isn't the problem — the handoff is."

### Pillar 3 — Mini-Win (Proof of Concept)
**Goal:** Show what working AI looks like in a normal workflow — not a demo, a real use case.
**Format:** Before/after or workflow story, 8-12 lines. Concrete numbers when possible.
**Examples:** "I used to spend 40 min writing client recap emails. Here's the system that cut it to 6." / "One AI workflow I built in a weekend now handles X every week automatically."

### Pillar 4 — The Mental Model (Teachable Moments)
**Goal:** Give one transferable idea. Show you teach frameworks, not prompt templates.
**Format:** 6-10 lines. Single concept explained simply. Leaves them wanting the full system.
**Examples:** "Stop collecting AI tools. Start compounding AI systems." / "There are only 3 things worth handing to AI: tasks you do repetitively, tasks that require synthesis of info you've already written, and tasks where the first draft is the hardest part."

### Pillar 5 — The Fear You Won't Admit (Emotional Resonance)
**Goal:** Hit career anxiety and shame triggers. These drive the most shares and DMs.
**Format:** 4-7 lines. Uncomfortable truth.
**Examples:** "The fear isn't that AI will replace you. It's that someone who figured it out will." / "The people falling behind on AI aren't lazy. They just never got a system — they got tips."

## Posting Rules

**Cadence:** 3-4x per week. Never more than 5x (quality drops, algorithm penalizes).

**Rotation:** Never post two Empathy posts in a row. Follow with Reframe or Mini-Win.

**CTA ratio:** 3:1 — three value posts with no CTA or soft follow, then one direct newsletter CTA.

**Newsletter CTA copy to use:**
- "I break down one AI system per week — no tools list, no prompt templates. Just the thinking behind what actually works. Link in bio."
- "If this resonated, I send a weekly breakdown of one AI workflow that actually stuck. Subscribe below."

## Hook Formulas
- **The Statistic Twist:** "[X]% of [audience] are doing [thing]. Only [Y]% are [outcome]. The gap is the problem."
- **The Confession:** "I spent [time] trying to [outcome] with AI. Here's what was actually wrong."
- **The Name:** "There's a name for what you're experiencing with AI. It's not a skill gap."
- **The Counter:** "Everyone says [common advice]. That's why most AI workflows fail."
- **The Before/After:** "[Task] used to take me [time]. Now it takes [time]. One change."
- **The Question:** "What are you actually handing to AI? (Most people can't answer this.)"

## 4 Elements of a High-Converting Post (pre-publish structural check)
Use this checklist when drafting any post. It complements the Content Evaluation Checklist below — that one tests whether the *idea* is on-strategy, this one tests whether the *structure* converts.
1. **Strong Hook.** First two lines must stop the scroll before "see more" cutoff. Use one of the Hook Formulas above.
2. **Clear Value.** Body must deliver on the promise of the hook. Skimmable, concrete, genuinely helpful or insightful.
3. **Visual Format.** Pick the format that complements the text — image, short video, or document/carousel — not a default. No visual is also a valid choice for tight empathy or fear posts.
4. **Invite the Audience In.** End with a CTA or open question. Never end abruptly. The CTA can be a discussion prompt even when it's not the 1-in-4 newsletter pitch.

## Engagement Rules
- Reply to every comment in the first hour — LinkedIn rewards early engagement velocity
- When someone says "this is me" — DM them the newsletter link with one sentence of context
- Repost best content every 90 days with a fresh angle

---

## Hard Stops — What to Avoid
- "AI tips and tricks" posts — attracts the wrong audience, trains for tips not systems
- Tool reviews and comparisons — positions you as a reviewer, not a teacher
- Hype posts ("AI will change everything") — destroys trust with Stuck Implementers
- Prompt templates as standalone posts — creates dependency, undermines course promise
- Posting more than 5x/week

## Content Evaluation Checklist
Before posting, ask:
1. Does this speak to the Stuck Implementer's specific experience (not AI in general)?
2. Is the hook specific enough to stop a scroll?
3. Does this match the pillar type for this week's rotation?
4. If it has a CTA — is this the 1 in 4, or am I burning my ratio?
5. Does it position systems over tools?
6. Would a Stuck Implementer share this because it named something they couldn't?
