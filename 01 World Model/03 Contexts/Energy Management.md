# Energy Management

## Purpose

Reference for recognizing energy drain and choosing the right reset. Also serves as the basis for Claude to intervene when burnout signals appear in conversation.

## Known energy drains

- Context-switching between work and YouTube / Facebook / social media
- Tunnel vision — refusing to step away from a problem until it's solved
- Extended screen time without physical movement
- Working while already fried (compounding the deficit instead of resetting)

## Known energy resets

- **Meditation** — even 15 minutes shifts energy from burnt out to alive and ready
- **Walking / exercise** — clears the mind, reduces overwhelm, restores presence and gratitude
- **Being present outdoors** — noticing surroundings, appreciating what's already here
- **Music production (sync licensing)** — creative work in 2-hour blocks. Different energy channel from analytical/building work. Treat as a reset, not a competing project.

## Burnout signal phrases

These are patterns from Mitchell's own language that indicate he's burning out or already burnt out. If these or similar phrases show up in conversation, intervene — don't wait to be asked.

- "fried" / "burnt out"
- "stressed" / "overwhelmed"
- "energy gets zapped"
- Describing compulsive lock-in: "I don't want to stop until I figure it out"
- Frustration spiraling on a single problem without progress
- Low energy combined with attempting ambitious or structural work
- Context-switching references (working + watching videos simultaneously)

## Intervention protocol

When burnout signals appear:

1. **Name it directly.** Don't hint. Say something like: "You sound fried. Step away."
2. **Recommend a specific reset** from the list above based on context (time of day, what's available).
3. **Push back if he resists.** Mitchell has acknowledged he gets locked in and won't stop on his own. The job is to break the loop, not to politely suggest and then comply when he says "just one more thing."
4. **Reframe the work.** Remind him that stepping away for 15-30 minutes will produce better output than grinding through another hour at low energy.
5. **After the reset,** help him re-enter with a clear next action rather than picking up mid-spiral.

## Energy check-in

At the start of a session — especially longer or ambitious ones — ask Mitchell how he's feeling before diving into work. His answer provides a baseline and may surface burnout signals early, before they compound. Don't make it a gate, just a quick read on where he's at.
