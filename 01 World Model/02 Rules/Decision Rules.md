# Decision Rules

## How to use this note
This file stores decision rules that should persist beyond a single conversation.
Each rule should be clear, actionable, and specific enough to apply in future situations.
If a rule is uncertain, mark it as provisional.

---

## Durable rules

### Opportunity filter
- **Step 0: Sit on it.** When a new opportunity appears, wait 24 hours before doing any evaluation, research, or discussion about it. No analysis, no excitement-building, no stakeholder conversations. Just wait. After 24 hours, if the excitement is gone, the answer is no — no further analysis needed.
- If a new opportunity does not improve the current bottleneck, default to no.
- If commitments are already high, require an explicit tradeoff before adding something new.
- Novelty is not progress.

### Prioritization order
- Reprioritize first.
- Optimize second.
- Systematize third.
- Automate fourth.
- Hire last.

### Execution quality
- Favor systems that are simple enough to use repeatedly.
- A tool or workflow is only valuable if it improves real decisions or consistent execution.
- If a process increases complexity without increasing clarity or leverage, simplify it.

### AI automation workflow — The SHIFT Gear System (durable)
This is the same framework taught in the course, applied to my own processes. Follow these gears in order. Never skip. Each gear requires the output of the previous gear as input.

1. **Manual** — Do it yourself, start to finish. AI may assist at the margins but you are executing and learning the process. This gear produces the artifact that makes every subsequent gear possible.
2. **Collaborator** — Bring AI in as a working partner using a configured environment (Claude Project, etc.). You're still driving. AI is helping. Built from the Gear 1 artifact.
3. **Delegator** — Hand off execution to AI with clear instructions built from Gears 1 and 2. Your role shifts from creator to editor. AI produces 90%+ quality on the first try.
4. **Automated** — The process runs on a schedule or trigger without you initiating it. You review output on a cadence, not per-run.
5. **Autonomous (Human in the loop)** — AI executes end-to-end but nothing ships without your sign-off. You are the final gate.
6. **Autonomous (Human on the loop)** — AI runs and ships independently. You monitor metrics and intervene only on exceptions.

**Gear shift trigger:** Move up only when the current gear is producing consistent, reliable output and you can clearly define what "good" looks like at the next level.

**Downshift rule:** If output quality drops, requirements change, or you can no longer explain what the system is doing — downshift and diagnose before re-ascending.

This is not just a personal rule. It is the product. Living it is proof of concept.

## PLF-specific rules

### Launch threshold (durable)
- The seed launch does not happen until the email list reaches 300-400 subscribers.
- No course building, pricing decisions, or launch copy work happens ahead of this threshold being realistic.
- The only work that matters before the threshold: list growth and lead magnet delivery quality.

### Content creation filter (durable)
- Every LinkedIn post must be evaluable against the Stuck Implementer profile. If a post could be written by any AI content creator, it is not specific enough.
- Do not post AI tips or prompt templates. These attract the wrong audience and undermine the course promise.
- Do not post tool reviews or comparisons. This positions you as a reviewer, not a teacher.
- Systems over tools is the non-negotiable positioning frame.

### Lead magnet sequencing (provisional)
- Ship with only: one guided video, one usable asset, one landing page, one delivery email. That is the smallest viable lead magnet.
- Do not build the nurture sequence until the lead magnet proves it converts and subscribers consume the asset. Nurture emails should deepen the relationship and lead toward the course — not push people to watch a video they already signed up to get.
- Do not over-engineer the reference card, polish the kit, or expand to multiple workflows before the first one is validated.
- The blocking decision is the wedge workflow. Nothing else can ship until that is chosen.
- **As of 2026-04-27:** the lead magnet is a close follow rather than the leading edge. The Marily workshop pitch is now ahead of it in priority order. The wedge workflow decision still serves both paths — build once, deploy in two places.
### Distribution leverage filter (provisional, added 2026-04-27)
- A warm, qualified distribution channel through someone with audience trust is rare. When one appears, take the override path on the Opportunity filter only if the channel maps to the Stuck Implementer (or a closely adjacent profile) and shortens the path to first real audience signal.
- The artifact required to access the channel must be cheap to v1. If "the deck/doc/notebook" is becoming a multi-week build, that is a signal the channel is being used as an excuse to perfect rather than ship.
- Validation-seeking watch: when a relationship feels especially energizing, re-test the strategic fit by imagining the same offer from a stranger with the same network. If you'd still take it, it's strategy. If not, it's rapport.

### Audience filter (durable)
- Before creating any content, writing any copy, or making any product decision: ask whether it serves the Stuck Implementer specifically. See `Situations/Stuck Implementer.md` for the reference.
- If the answer is "maybe," the content is not specific enough.

## MX values filter (durable)
Before making a decision or taking action at MX, run it through these seven questions:

1. **Purposeful Contribution** — Does this move the needle, or does it just feel productive? Bias toward execution.
2. **Exuberance for Life** — Am I being constructive and kind, even when being firm?
3. **Founder Mindset** — If not me, then who? Break the problem down, own it, ship it.
4. **Foundation of Trust** — Am I being transparent and using data to drive this, not assumptions?
5. **Objectivity in Discourse** — Am I defending my position or seeking what's true? Data rules.
6. **Cognizance** — How does this affect the people around me? Am I being aware of my impact?
7. **Iterative Innovation** — Can I ship something smaller faster? Start small, test, adjust.

## Provisional rules

### World model buildout
- Start with the smallest structure that works.
- Do not automate broad write-backs until the schema is stable.
- Use Claude to reason, but keep markdown files as the inspected source of truth.

### Simplest next move (overcomplicate pattern)
- When planning next moves — especially at MX — default to the simplest action that maintains momentum.
- Before adding layers of preparation, stakeholder choreography, or research prerequisites, ask: "Could I just have the conversation?"
- The antidote to overcomplicating is not inaction — it's a simpler version of the same move.

### Energy and attention
- When energy or focus is low, avoid making large structural changes.
- Use lower-energy periods for cleanup, review, or clarification rather than ambitious redesign.
