# Solo Building — Zombie Projects and Domain Fit

## Zombie project diagnostic

The worst outcome isn't zero revenue — zero is at least a clear signal. The worst outcome is a zombie project: low but non-zero traction after months of grinding.

**The test:** Real traction feels alive — like the product is carrying you. If it feels like you're dragging a lifeless body uphill, constantly pumping life into it, kill it. Pack up the lessons and move on.

## Marketing difficulty as product signal

If marketing feels brutally hard all the time, the uncomfortable answer is often that the product isn't strong enough yet. A great product multiplies every marketing effort. A weak product makes every channel feel broken.

The cope to watch for: blaming reach, algorithms, bad luck, or insufficient marketing knowledge instead of asking whether the product is useful enough.

## Domain experience over random ideas

Transfer real domain experience into what you build. Pick something where you understand the users, the pain, the language, the tradeoffs, the taste, the signals. Real life experience is the edge — not what looks profitable from the outside.

## Pick a lane and compound

Build in the same domain so lessons carry forward. When one project fails, you're not back to zero — you carry insights, taste, and understanding into the next thing. Multiple products in the same lane compound; random bets don't.
