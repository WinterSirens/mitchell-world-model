## Post Body

A lot of what people call "AI efficiency" is just laziness with extra steps.

I think there's a real distinction worth naming, because the trap is subtle.

Lazy is handing something to AI just because you can. It feels like delegation. But if you're spending more time prompting, re-prompting, reviewing, and editing than you would have just doing the task, you haven't saved anything. You've just moved the friction.

Efficient is removing a friction point that was actually holding you back.

Here's a concrete example of the difference.

I want to publish a thought on LinkedIn. Old path: write it out, format it, open the scheduling tool, configure the post, hit send. Four handoffs. That friction is exactly why ideas die in draft folders.

New path: I think through the idea on a walk, talk it out, throw it into Claude Code, and it formats and schedules the post. I never had to context-switch into "LinkedIn mode."

I didn't remove the thinking. I removed the friction between the thought and the post going live.

That's not lazy. That's efficient.

The question I ask before automating anything now: am I removing a real friction point, or am I outsourcing something because I don't feel like doing it?

If it's the second one, it's usually faster to just do it myself.

What's a workflow you've tried to hand off to AI that ended up taking more time than it saved?
