## Post Body

You can build anything as an AI product manager. That's exactly why so many AI products fail.

I just wrapped Maralee Nika's AI PM bootcamp, and watching 15 working prototypes on graduation day made one thing obvious.

The teams who struggled stayed married to their solution. Every pivot felt like a loss. Every piece of feedback that didn't land felt like a setback.

The teams who were solid weren't attached to what they built. They were attached to the problem they were solving.

The frame I'm taking away: fall in love with the problem, not the solution.

When the problem is your north star, the solution is just your current best answer. You can change it without losing direction. Pivoting isn't failure. It's updating your answer.

The hard part for most people building AI products right now is that the tools are genuinely capable enough to build almost anything quickly. That abundance is a trap if you don't know what problem you're actually solving.

The real constraint isn't what you can build. It's whether you understand the problem well enough to know when you've actually solved it.

There's no shortage of problems to go discover. I'm excited to go find them.

What problem are you building toward right now, and can you articulate it without mentioning your solution?
