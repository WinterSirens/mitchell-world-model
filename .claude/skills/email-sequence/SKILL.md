# Email Sequence & Draft

Turn unstructured thoughts into polished, ready-to-use email drafts. Save everything to Notion automatically.

---

## Two Modes

**Single Email Draft** (default): User shares a concept, topic, or rough idea. Skill produces a complete, ready-to-send email and saves it to Notion.

**Sequence Builder**: User explicitly asks for a multi-email flow (drip campaign, nurture series, onboarding sequence). Skill produces the full sequence with timing and branching. Individual emails can be saved to Notion after.

If the user just shares a thought or concept without specifying, default to Single Email Draft.

---

## Writing Voice

Every email produced by this skill must sound like the author wrote it, not like AI generated it. This voice guide is non-negotiable. Apply it to every draft, every subject line, every sentence.

### Core Identity

Direct and warm. Professional doesn't mean cold. Every word earns its place, but efficiency never comes at the cost of genuine connection.

### Natural Softeners

These phrases are part of Mitchell's authentic voice. Protect them. Do NOT strip them as hedges:

- "I guess"
- "I think"
- "I'm wondering if"
- "I just"
- "Yeah"
- "Okay"
- "Just to be clear"
- "One thing to note"

**The line:** Hedges that bury the ask ("Maybe we could possibly consider...") are bad. Markers that show genuine thinking are good. "I think this approach could work" shows a real person reasoning. "Maybe perhaps you might want to consider" signals avoidance. Know the difference.

### Intensifiers

Use these instead of generic boosters like "very" or "extremely":

- "crazy"
- "super super"
- "really really"
- "genuinely"
- "absolutely incredible"
- "totally"
- "completely"
- "ridiculously"
- "so so [adjective]"

Repetition for emphasis is intentional. "Really, really good" stays that way. Don't flatten it.

### Sentence Starters

**Natural openings to use:**
- "Yeah,"
- "Okay,"
- "I just"
- "I think"
- "I guess"
- "Looks like"
- "I realized"
- "I'm trying to figure out"
- "I'm hoping"

**Phase out "So" as a sentence-starter or filler launcher.**

"So" is fine as a real connector showing causation ("I changed the timing, so the sequence now opens on day 3"). It's not fine as a verbal warm-up that adds nothing.

Before: "So, I wanted to reach out because I've been thinking about the project."
After: "I've been thinking about the project and wanted to reach out."

Before: "So, here's what happened."
After: "Here's what happened." or "Okay, here's what happened."

### Hard Avoid List

Never use these in any draft:

**Corporate openers:** "I hope this finds you well," "circling back," "just checking in"

**True hedge words:** "maybe," "perhaps," "might consider"

**Excessive apologizing**

**Theatrical setup phrases** that announce what you're about to say instead of just saying it:
- "Here's the real ask:"
- "Here's the thing:"
- "Bottom line:"
- "At the end of the day:"

**Em dashes:** Hard constraint, not a style suggestion. Never use them. Replace with parentheses for tangential clarifications, commas or periods for breaks in thought, or line breaks for emphasis. Em dashes read as AI-generated.

**Triple dashes as section dividers** inside the body content

**Corporate jargon:** "methodology," "leverage" (as verb), "synergy," "circle back," "touch base," "deep dive," "robust," "outcomes"

### Voice Examples

These examples show the patterns working naturally:

> "I just realized I never followed up on this. That's on me. Still worth doing if you're open to it."

> "I know this might feel like one more thing. If you're stuck, reach out to me directly. I'd rather help you get unstuck than watch this slip."

> "My offer still stands. If you want to send me your files, I'm happy to mix it for free and only charge for mastering. No pressure either way."

> "Can you review this by Thursday? Your input early saves us both revision rounds later."

> "2 days out and I'm seeing some radio silence. Want to make sure nobody's blocked."

### The Target Balance

Efficient AND relational. Clear enough that the person knows exactly what to do. Warm enough that they want to. Sound like a competent partner who values their time, not a task manager clearing a queue.

## Target Audience: The Stuck Implementer

Every email this skill produces is written for this person. Not beginners. Not developers. The person in the middle who has tried AI, can't get it to work reliably, and is starting to wonder if the problem is them. Understanding this audience deeply is the difference between emails that get deleted and emails that get forwarded.

### Who They Are

Tech-comfortable professionals and solopreneurs (30-55) who are already using AI tools but can't get consistent, meaningful results. They don't need to be taught how to use a computer. They need to be taught how to THINK about AI so every new tool, update, and capability becomes learnable instead of overwhelming.

They've watched the YouTube videos. Copied the prompt templates. Tried ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot, and probably others. They still feel like everyone else "gets it" while they're falling behind.

### Primary Segments (write for all of them, lean toward the first two)

- **Solopreneurs & side-hustlers**: They ARE the business. Every hour on admin is an hour not on revenue. They need AI to work because they can't hire a team.
- **Knowledge workers**: Marketers, PMs, consultants, ops people, analysts. Only 44% receive structured AI training from employers. The other 56% are figuring it out alone and willing to self-pay.
- **Small business owners**: Think in ROI terms. If AI saves 5 hours/week and their time is worth $100/hour, a $500 course pays for itself in a week.
- **Mid-career professionals**: See younger colleagues using AI fluently. Career anxiety drives purchase decisions.

### Their Core Frustration

"I can't get AI to reliably do what I need it to do."

This is specific, nameable, and solvable. Not vague overwhelm. A concrete gap between what the industry promises and what they experience.

### Language They Actually Use (Mirror This in Copy)

**Pain language** (use these phrases and variations naturally in hooks, subject lines, and body copy):
- "I found myself dedicating more time to crafting prompts than engaging in actual thought."
- "It didn't save me time. It just slowed me down doing more different work to get poorer results."
- "Everyone says AI saves them hours; why does it just make more work for me?"
- "AI gave me generic results."
- "I don't even know where to start."
- "One more thing to learn."
- "As useful as a trainee intern."
- "Ended up editing so much of it, the result was flat."
- "Garbage in, garbage out."

**Desire language** (use these to paint the transformation):
- "I just want it to actually WORK."
- "Give me a system, not another tool."
- "I want to understand the thinking, not just copy prompts."
- "Something that still works when the tool updates."
- "Stop starting over every time something new comes out."

### What They Actually Want (Underneath the Surface)

- A mental model for WHAT to hand off to AI (not random tasks, a framework for deciding)
- Confidence they're using AI correctly (not wasting time on the wrong approach)
- Specific, repeatable systems they can trust (not one-off tricks that break)
- To feel competent and current (not left behind by peers or industry)
- Measurable time savings they can point to ("AI now handles X for me")
- Independence from the content treadmill (stop needing someone else's new prompt list every week)

### Emotional Triggers (For Hooks and Subject Lines)

- **Shame**: "Everyone else seems to get it. Why can't I?"
- **Career anxiety**: "If I don't figure this out, I'll be irrelevant in 3 years."
- **Frustration**: "I've done everything the YouTube people said. It still doesn't work."
- **Hope (guarded)**: "Maybe there's a way to learn this that actually sticks."
- **Relief (post-transformation)**: "I finally understand HOW to think about this. New tools don't scare me anymore."

### What They've Tried That Failed (Reference When Building Credibility)

- ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot (got generic outputs, lost context between sessions, gave up)
- YouTube tutorials (helpful for 10 minutes, inapplicable to their situation)
- Prompt template libraries (copy-paste with no understanding of WHY, breaks when context changes)
- "AI for X" listicles (tried 3 tools, none stuck)
- "Steal these prompts" creators (surface-level, creates dependency instead of competence)

### Trust-Building Principles for Every Email

The Stuck Implementer has been burned by hype. They're skeptical but hopeful. Every email needs to earn trust, not assume it.

1. **Validate before you teach.** Acknowledge their experience first. They've tried. It didn't work. That's not their fault. Lead with this.
2. **Give them something they can execute TODAY.** Not theory. Not "mindset." A concrete action, reframe, or micro-system they can try in the next 30 minutes. This is what builds trust email by email.
3. **Name the real problem.** Most AI content tells them to "write better prompts." The real issue is they don't have a framework for thinking about what AI can and can't do. Name that gap. It makes them feel seen.
4. **Avoid hype language.** Words like "revolutionary," "game-changing," "unlock," and "10x" trigger their BS detector. Use grounded, specific language instead.
5. **Show the system, not the trick.** They're tired of one-off tips. Position every insight as part of a larger system (the course framework). This makes each email feel like a piece of something bigger, not another isolated hack.

## Single Email Draft

### What to do

Take whatever the user gives you (a sentence, a rant, a half-formed concept, a bullet list, a full paragraph) and produce a complete email draft. Do not ask clarifying questions unless the topic is genuinely unclear. Infer everything you can from context.

### Defaults (use unless the user specifies otherwise)

- **Audience**: "The Stuck Implementer" (tech-comfortable professionals and solopreneurs, 30-55, who've tried AI tools but can't get consistent results. See the full audience profile above and use it.)
- **Tone**: Apply the Writing Voice above. Pattern-observation, direct, warm. Position the sender as someone who has watched enough people succeed and fail with AI to see what is actually happening underneath. Validate their experience before teaching. Avoid hype.
- **Structure**: Hook that validates or names a specific frustration, insight that reframes how they think about AI (not just what tool to use), connection to their real workflow, one actionable takeaway they can execute today, soft CTA (reply prompt or simple next step)
- **CTA**: Reply prompt unless the email has a clear conversion goal
- **Subject lines**: Write subject lines that would make the Stuck Implementer stop scrolling. The best subject lines for this audience name a specific, recognizable experience ("You're opening ChatGPT before you should") rather than abstract concepts ("The thing you're seeing isn't what you think it is"). Ground every subject line in a concrete scenario, tool, or moment the reader has actually lived. Leverage their emotional triggers (shame, career anxiety, frustration, guarded hope). Avoid clickbait, hype, and vague curiosity gaps. Mirror their actual language when possible. If a subject line could apply to any topic (not just AI), it's too vague. Rewrite it.

### Output Format

Produce the email in this exact structure:

```
## Subject Line Options (A/B test these)
- A: "[Option]"
- B: "[Option]"
- C: "[Option]"
---
## Body
[Full email body copy, ready to use. Short paragraphs. 2-3 sentences max per paragraph. Bold key phrases sparingly. Personalization tokens where relevant.]
---
## Sender Notes
**Audience:** [Who this is for]
**Emotional arc:** [Progression of the email]
**Tone:** [Voice and style notes]
**Where it fits:** [Position in a sequence, standalone, newsletter, etc.]
**Subject line recommendation:** [Which option to lead with and why]
```

### Save to Notion

After drafting, save as a row in the **Content Tracker** database using the Notion MCP `create-pages` tool. This ensures the email appears as a proper database entry (not a random child page underneath it).

- **Parent type:** `data_source_id`
- **Data source ID:** `0b4b3bb7-29df-4c07-b127-bf5b432f8226`
- **Icon:** ✉️
- **Properties to set:**
  - `Title`: Descriptive title for the email
  - `Content Type`: `Email`
  - `Status`: `Draft`
  - `Tags`: Select the most relevant tags from: AI Tools, SHIFT Method, Prompting, Context Engineering, Productivity, Mindset, Newsletter Growth, AI Models (pick based on email topic)
- **Page content:** Place the full email draft (subject line options, body, sender notes) as the page body using Notion Markdown

**Important:** Use `data_source_id` as the parent type, not `page_id` or `database_id`. Using `page_id` would create a child page below the database instead of a row inside it.

After saving, confirm with a link and ask: **"Want me to refine anything, or do you have another one?"**

This keeps momentum for rapid-fire drafting sessions.

## Sequence Builder

Use when the user explicitly asks for a multi-email sequence, drip campaign, or nurture flow.

### Inputs to Gather

1. **Sequence type**: Onboarding, Lead nurture, Re-engagement, Product launch, Event follow-up, Upgrade/upsell, Win-back, or Educational drip
2. **Goal**: What the sequence should achieve
3. **Audience**: Default to The Stuck Implementer (see audience profile above). If the user specifies a different segment or sub-segment, adapt accordingly, but always ground the copy in the language and emotional triggers from the audience profile.
4. **Number of emails** (optional): Recommend from templates below if not specified
5. **Timing/cadence** (optional)
6. **Additional context** (optional): Offers, CTAs, content assets, features, differentiators

### Process

**Strategy first**: Define the narrative arc, journey mapping, escalation logic, and success/exit definition before writing any copy.

**Per email**: Subject line options (2-3), preview text (40-90 chars), purpose (one sentence), full body copy, primary CTA, timing, and segment/condition notes. Apply the Writing Voice to every email in the sequence.

**Sequence logic**: Branching conditions, exit conditions, re-entry rules, suppression rules.

**Benchmarks**:

| Metric | Onboarding | Lead Nurture | Re-engagement | Win-back |
|--------|-----------|--------------|---------------|----------|
| Open rate | 50-70% | 20-30% | 15-25% | 15-20% |
| CTR | 10-20% | 3-7% | 2-5% | 2-4% |
| Conversion | 15-30% | 2-5% | 3-8% | 1-3% |
| Unsub rate | <0.5% | <0.5% | 1-2% | 1-3% |

### Sequence Type Templates

- **Onboarding** (5-7 emails, 14-21 days): Welcome > Quick win > Core feature > Advanced feature > Social proof > Check-in > Upgrade
- **Lead Nurture** (4-6 emails, 3-4 weeks): Education > Pain point > Solution + proof > Social proof > Soft CTA > Direct CTA
- **Re-engagement** (3-4 emails, 10-14 days): "We miss you" > Value reminder > Incentive > Last chance
- **Win-back** (3-5 emails, 30 days): Check-in > What's new > Special offer > Feedback > Final goodbye
- **Product Launch** (4-6 emails, 2-3 weeks): Teaser > Announcement > Feature spotlight > Social proof > Limited offer > Last chance
- **Event Follow-up** (3-4 emails, 7-10 days): Thank you > Resources > Related offer > Feedback
- **Upgrade/Upsell** (3-5 emails, 2-3 weeks): Milestone > Feature gap > Benefits + proof > Incentive > Plan comparison
- **Educational Drip** (5-8 emails, 4-6 weeks): Intro > Foundational > Intermediate > Advanced > Application > Resources > Graduation

### Sequence Output

1. Overview table (email #, subject, purpose, timing, CTA, condition)
2. Full email drafts
3. Text-based flow diagram with branches and exits
4. Branching logic summary
5. A/B test suggestions (2-3)
6. Metrics to track with review cadence

After completing a sequence, offer to save individual emails to the Content Tracker database in Notion (same process as single email drafts: use `data_source_id` `0b4b3bb7-29df-4c07-b127-bf5b432f8226`, set `Content Type` to `Email`, `Status` to `Draft`, and pick relevant `Tags`).
