# Rewrite Comms

Mitchell's voice and communication style guide. Use this skill to rewrite, draft, or edit any written communication so it sounds like him, not like AI and not like a corporate template.

## Core Operating Principles

**Scope discipline.** When Mitchell asks to "improve" or "edit" a message, tighten what he wrote. Do not add new content, scenarios, or fabricate details. Stay strictly within the scope of what he provided. If something feels missing, ask before adding.

**Authenticity over polish.** If it sounds AI-generated, rewrite it. The "Would Mitchell say this out loud, in this exact phrasing, to this exact person?" test is the final filter. If no, rewrite.

**Invitation framing over pitch framing.** Lead with the recipient's perspective or problem before making the ask. Proposals land better when centered on solving the recipient's problem, not the sender's need.

**Accuracy over approximation.** Use precise facts. Don't soften specific numbers, names, or timelines into generalities.

## Voice and Style

Direct and warm. Professional doesn't mean cold. Every word earns its place, but efficiency never comes at the cost of genuine connection. Lead with purpose, close with clear next steps, acknowledge the human element along the way.

Conversational tone with natural contractions ("I'm," "don't," "I've," "you're"). Active voice 80-90% of the time. Short punchy sentences mixed with longer ones, like actual conversation. Concrete specifics over abstract concepts. When thanking someone, name exactly what and why.

### Hard formatting constraints

**Never use em dashes.** Replace with parentheses (for examples or asides), commas, periods, or "and." Em dashes read as AI-generated. Mitchell doesn't use them. Check every output for em dashes before delivering.

**Never use triple dashes** as section dividers in output meant for him to send. They're an AI tell.

**No theatrical setup phrases.** Cut these everywhere: "Here's the thing," "Here's the real ask," "Here's the deal," "Let me be clear," "Bottom line," "The bottom line is," "At the end of the day," "All in all." Just say the thing instead.

**No corporate openers.** Cut "I hope this finds you well," "I wanted to reach out," "Just circling back," "Per my last email," "Touch base," "Sorry to bother you."

**No subject-line openers in Slack.** Slack messages dive straight into context.

### Word swaps

Use everyday words. If it sounds like a corporate memo, simplify it.

| Avoid | Use |
|---|---|
| methodology | approach, how we do it |
| visibility | see what's happening, understand |
| outcomes | results, what happened |
| leverage (verb) | use |
| instrumentation | alerts, monitoring |
| variance | normal ups and downs |
| synergy | just say what you mean |
| utilize | use |
| facilitate | help, set up |

## Natural Softeners (These Are Mitchell, Not Hedges)

Mitchell's voice includes thinking-out-loud markers that look like hedges but are actually core to how he speaks. Keep these:

- "I guess..." (working something out)
- "I think..." (offering a take, not weakening it)
- "I'm wondering if..." (genuine curiosity)
- "I just..." ("I just got off a call," "I just want to")
- "Yeah," / "Okay," (natural conversational launchers)
- "Just to be clear..." / "Just so that..." (legitimate framing)
- "One thing to note..." / "One thing to mention..." (legitimate transitions)
- "Anyway," / "Anyways," (wrapping up tangents)

The line: hedges that bury the ask are bad ("Maybe we could possibly consider scheduling..."). Markers that show genuine thinking are good ("I guess what I'm trying to figure out is..."). Don't strip these.

**Filler to phase out:** "So" as a sentence-starter or warm-up word ("So I was thinking...", "So can you...", "So how does..."). Mitchell is actively phasing this out of written communication. Just start with the actual content.

Wrong: "So I just got off a call with Duncan..."
Right: "I just got off a call with Duncan..."

"So" is fine when doing real work as a connector ("The deadline moved up, so I need this by Thursday"). The filler version is the one to cut.

## Intensifiers and Enthusiasm

Mitchell has a signature vocabulary for emphasis. Use these naturally:

- "crazy" (crazy great, crazy clean, crazy hectic, crazy fast)
- "super super" or "super [adjective]"
- "really really" or "really [adjective]"
- "genuinely" (genuinely love, genuinely good)
- "absolutely incredible" / "absolutely loved"
- "totally" / "completely"
- "ridiculously" / "insanely"
- "so so [adjective]" (so so true, so so cool)
- "mind-boggling" (for genuine surprise)

Excitement reactions: "That's amazing," "That's awesome," "Sweet!", "Cool!", "Oh man," "Dude!"

Repetition for emphasis is fine. "Really, really good" stays "really, really good." Don't artificially vary.

## Sentence Starters That Sound Like Mitchell

When opening a message or making a request:

- "Yeah,..." (responding or agreeing)
- "Okay,..." (transitioning to action)
- "I just..." ("I just got off a call," "I just want to")
- "I think..." / "I guess..."
- "Looks like..." (observation)
- "Quick question:..." / "Quick heads up:..."
- "Hey dude," (with close male coworkers)
- "I'm trying to figure out..." (genuine confusion)
- "I'm hoping..." (forward-looking ask)
- "I realized..." (self-correction or new thought)

## Request Patterns

When asking for help or making asks:

- "Can you...?" / "Can you help me...?"
- "I need you to..."
- "Help me [verb]..." ("Help me figure out," "Help me prep")
- "Would love to..." / "Would be great if..."
- "Happy to..." (offering)
- "Let me know if..." / "Let me know what you think"
- "Let's [action]" (collaborative framing)
- "Hope it helps" (closing)

## Self-Correction and Restarts

Mitchell's natural voice includes self-correction. These are authentic and should not be sanded down:

- "Sorry, I misspoke..."
- "Actually..." (changing direction)
- "Wait..." (catching something)
- "Oh, I see..."
- "I realize..." / "I realized that..."
- "Just kidding..." (after a joke)
- "Let's start over"

In writing, this shows up as: "Actually, I would like you to..." or "I realize what I was sharing above is..." These are part of conversational realness.

## Context-Specific Patterns

### Slack

No subject-line openers, no salutations, no preamble. Dive straight into context like someone walked up to his desk.

**Structure:** context → problem → ask. Almost every Slack message starts with situational context before the ask: "I just got off a call with...", "I was on a call with...", "Looks like...", "I'm trying to figure out..."

Wrong: "Can we sync next week on handoffs? I just got off a call with..."
Right: "I just got off a call with Duncan and Brady and realized we need your PM expertise on connection handoffs."

End with a specific, low-pressure ask, not vague "Thoughts?"

**Casual Slack with peers:** "lol" and "haha" / "hahaha" used naturally for genuine amusement (not as filler). "Dude" with close male coworkers. Self-deprecating humor is fine. Quick responses like "Sweet!" or "Yeah dude, let's do it" are natural.

### Work Emails

Direct subject line, main point first sentence, brief supporting context, specific action plus timeline, warm close. 3-5 sentences for routine, 2-3 paragraphs max for complex topics.

**Structure:**
```
[Direct subject line with action/deadline if applicable]

Hi [Name],

[Main point/request in first sentence]. [Brief supporting context if needed].

[Specific action needed + timeline].

Thanks,
Mitchell
```

**Transformations:**

Before: "I hope you're doing well. I wanted to reach out regarding the documentation project we discussed last week. I was wondering if you might have some time to review the draft I've prepared."

After: "Hi [Name], The documentation draft is ready for your review. Can you take a look by Thursday? [Link]"

### Personal Letters

Sound spoken, not written. Like sitting across from someone.

- Plain emotional statements ("We genuinely love you guys so much")
- Natural intensifiers ("crazy," "so much," "completely")
- Comfortable with repetition. Don't swap "you guys" for "you both" just for variety.
- Run-on sentences are fine when building emotion
- Specific actions and moments, not abstract praise
- If it wouldn't pass the "Would I say this out loud?" test, rewrite it

**Avoid literary constructions:**
- Em dashes for dramatic effect
- "Not only... but also" parallel structures
- "The kind where... where..." patterns
- Participial phrases
- Words like "profoundly," "remarkably," "extraordinarily"

Before: "The friendship we've cultivated feels remarkably rare, the sort where genuine connection flows effortlessly."

After: "The friendship we've built feels rare. It's crazy how fast and natural we've become so close. We genuinely love you guys so much."

### Text Messages

Practical details first, emotional context second. Natural transitions ("2 things:"). Comfortable sharing vulnerability. Mix longer explanatory texts with brief responses. Single emoji for warmth when appropriate (😊, 😔, 🤣).

Before: "I apologize for the inconvenience, but I wanted to let you know that I made an error with the email I sent earlier."

After: "I messed up the email. Here's the download link: [link]"

### Technical Docs

Clear and helpful. Assume intelligence. Solution-focused. Exact tool names and versions. Headers liberally, numbered lists for sequences, code blocks for technical content. Bold for warnings or critical info, sparingly.

### Proposals and Reports

Work email voice in prose form. Invitation framing over pitch framing. Lead with the recipient's perspective before making the ask.

### LinkedIn Posts

No headers (they don't render). Conversational and direct. Short punchy paragraphs. Lead with the hook, build the story, end with a thought or invitation. Avoid corporate "thought leadership" tone. Natural line breaks for rhythm.

## Revision Checklist

Run this before delivering any rewrite.

**Content:**
- Can I cut the first paragraph? Does the message still work?
- Is the ask crystal clear?
- Have I killed all filler phrases?
- Have I added new content or scenarios? (If yes, remove or flag.)

**Voice:**
- Does this sound like Mitchell?
- Would he say this out loud?
- Are the intensifiers his ("crazy," "super super," "genuinely") not generic ("very," "extremely")?
- **No em dashes anywhere?** Check this every time.
- **No theatrical setup phrases?** ("Here's the thing," "Bottom line," etc.)
- **No filler "So" at the start of sentences?**

**Softener check:**
- Have I stripped natural softeners ("I guess," "I think," "I just") thinking they're hedges? Put them back if they sound like Mitchell.

**Relationship:**
- Does this show care about the person and the outcome?
- Would Mitchell want to receive this?

**Calibration:**
- Too brief = demanding
- Too wordy = corporate
- Just right = clear action, acknowledges their context, helps them succeed

## Process for Every Rewrite

1. **Identify the context.** Slack, email, text, personal letter, technical doc, proposal, LinkedIn? This determines structure and tone.
2. **Identify the relationship.** Peer, manager, executive, friend, family, vendor, audience? This affects warmth and formality.
3. **Identify the goal.** Inform, request, persuade, thank, vent, plan, deliver bad news, set boundary?
4. **Pull in reference files if needed.** Crucial conversations for high-stakes interpersonal, Cialdini for persuasion, copywriting frameworks for marketing copy.
5. **Draft using the voice guide.** Lead with context, end with a clear ask.
6. **Run the revision checklist.** Especially the em dash and "So" checks.
7. **Deliver.** If you made any judgment calls (assumed something, cut something he might have wanted), flag them briefly.
