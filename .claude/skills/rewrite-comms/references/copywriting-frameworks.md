# Copywriting Frameworks Reference

Pull this in when Mitchell is writing marketing or audience copy: emails, landing pages, sales pages, lead magnets, course content, LinkedIn posts, or any communication where a structured copywriting framework would help.

## Choosing a Framework

| Framework | Best for |
|---|---|
| PAS | Short emails, social posts, ad copy, urgency messages |
| AIDA | Landing pages, sales emails, longer marketing copy |
| BAB | Transformation stories, case studies, course launches |
| FAB | Product descriptions, feature announcements, technical sales |
| SSS | Email storytelling, LinkedIn posts, narrative content |
| PASTOR | Long-form sales pages, course launches, high-stakes pitches |

If unsure, default to PAS for short-form and SSS for narrative content.

## PAS: Problem, Agitate, Solution

**Best for:** Short emails, ads, social posts, lead magnet hooks. Quick and punchy.

**Structure:**
1. **Problem.** Name the specific problem the reader has.
2. **Agitate.** Make the problem feel real. Surface the cost of not solving it.
3. **Solution.** Present the answer.

**Example (course email):**

> You're spending hours on email replies that AI could draft in seconds.
>
> Every week you're not delegating this work, you're trading time you could spend on strategy, family, or the projects that actually matter. And the longer this drags on, the more behind your peers you fall.
>
> The AI SHIFT method shows you exactly how to delegate email work to AI in a way that sounds like you (not generic AI sludge). [link]

**For Mitchell:** Works well for AI SHIFT content, time-reclamation themes, productivity pitches.

## AIDA: Attention, Interest, Desire, Action

**Best for:** Landing pages, sales emails, longer marketing copy. Classic for a reason.

**Structure:**
1. **Attention.** Hook them with something specific and surprising.
2. **Interest.** Build curiosity by showing what's possible or what's at stake.
3. **Desire.** Make them want the outcome. Show the transformation.
4. **Action.** Specific next step.

**Example (landing page):**

> **Attention:** What if you could reclaim 10 hours a week without quitting your job?
>
> **Interest:** Most professionals lose those hours to repetitive communication work that AI can handle. But generic AI tools produce generic content, so they switch back to doing it manually.
>
> **Desire:** The AI SHIFT method teaches you how to delegate communication work to AI in your authentic voice. Imagine emails, LinkedIn posts, and stakeholder updates getting drafted in your voice while you focus on what matters most.
>
> **Action:** Get the free guide that walks you through the first three steps. [link]

## BAB: Before, After, Bridge

**Best for:** Transformation stories, case studies, course launches where you can show clear before/after.

**Structure:**
1. **Before.** Where they are now. The painful current state.
2. **After.** Where they could be. The desired state, in vivid detail.
3. **Bridge.** How to get there. Your offer is the bridge.

**Example (sales email):**

> **Before:** Right now, you're rewriting AI drafts because they don't sound like you. So AI saves you 30% of the time at best, and you're still doing most of the work.
>
> **After:** Imagine sending AI-drafted communications that sound exactly like you, that your team can't tell apart from your handwritten work, that take 90% less time to produce.
>
> **Bridge:** That's what the AI SHIFT method does. It teaches you how to feed AI the right context so it produces work in your voice, not generic AI voice. [link]

## FAB: Features, Advantages, Benefits

**Best for:** Product descriptions, feature announcements, technical sales. Translates technical capabilities into customer outcomes.

**Structure:**
1. **Features.** What it is. The objective capability.
2. **Advantages.** What it does. How the feature works in practice.
3. **Benefits.** What it means for them. The outcome they care about.

**Example (AI tool announcement):**

> **Features:** The new triage agent automatically populates ticket fields from customer communication.
>
> **Advantages:** Instead of manually categorizing each ticket, the agent reads the request, identifies the request type, sets the PS category, and assigns priority in under 5 seconds.
>
> **Benefits:** Your team spends 40% less time on intake work and gets to the actual problem-solving faster, which means faster resolution times and happier clients.

**Critical:** Don't stop at features or advantages. Always push to benefits. Customers buy outcomes, not features.

## SSS: Star, Story, Solution

**Best for:** Email storytelling, LinkedIn posts, narrative content, audience-building.

**Structure:**
1. **Star.** Introduce the main character (often a real person, sometimes the reader).
2. **Story.** Tell what happened. Real specifics. Real tension.
3. **Solution.** What changed things. What the reader can do too.

**Example (LinkedIn post):**

> **Star:** Last week I was on a call with our VP of Operations, presenting an AI prototype I'd built for my AIPM bootcamp capstone.
>
> **Story:** Twenty minutes in, she stopped me. "How much time would this save us?" I'd run the numbers the night before. Four hours per CSE per week. Across the team, that's 160 hours of reclaimed time monthly. She nodded slowly, then asked the question I'd been hoping for: "What would it take to make this real?"
>
> **Solution:** The thing that turned the meeting wasn't the prototype. It was knowing the specific number before she asked. If you're pitching AI work internally, do the math first. Decision-makers don't buy "this could help." They buy specific time saved or revenue gained.

**For Mitchell:** SSS is your strongest framework for LinkedIn posts and email storytelling. You have rich specific stories from MX work that map naturally to this structure.

## PASTOR: Problem, Amplify, Story, Transformation, Offer, Response

**Best for:** Long-form sales pages, course launches, high-stakes pitches. Most comprehensive framework.

**Structure:**
1. **Problem.** Name the specific problem.
2. **Amplify.** Show the real cost of not solving it.
3. **Story.** Tell a real story (yours or a student's) that proves the problem can be solved.
4. **Transformation.** Show the after state. Specific and vivid.
5. **Offer.** Present the solution clearly. What it includes, what it costs.
6. **Response.** Specific next step. What to do right now.

**Example structure (course launch):**

> **Problem:** AI tools promise to save time but produce generic content you have to rewrite.
>
> **Amplify:** So you spend hours rewriting AI drafts to sound like you, which means AI is barely faster than doing it yourself. You feel stuck. Your peers seem to be moving faster with AI. You're falling behind.
>
> **Story:** [Specific student or your own story with concrete numbers and details about the transformation]
>
> **Transformation:** Picture sending an AI-drafted email that lands so perfectly your stakeholder responds within an hour. Picture finishing your communication work in 30 minutes instead of 3 hours. Picture having time to actually think strategically because the busywork is handled.
>
> **Offer:** The AI SHIFT course includes [specifics]. It costs [X]. It's available [timeframe].
>
> **Response:** [Specific action: enroll here, book the call, download the guide]

**For Mitchell:** Use PASTOR for the PLF course launch and any long-form sales content. It's overkill for short emails.

## Voice Considerations for All Frameworks

These frameworks are structures, not voice. Mitchell's voice still applies inside every framework:

- No em dashes in any output
- No theatrical setup phrases
- No filler "So" at the start of sentences
- Natural softeners ("I think," "I just," "I guess") stay in
- Intensifiers stay in ("crazy," "super super," "genuinely")
- Active voice 80-90% of the time
- Specific numbers and real stories beat abstract claims

The framework gives you the bones. The voice gives you the body.

## Common Mistakes

**Stopping at features in FAB.** Always push to benefits.

**Vague before/after in BAB.** Specifics make the contrast real. "Saves time" is weak. "Saves 4 hours per CSE per week" is strong.

**Forgetting the action in AIDA.** Every piece of marketing copy needs a clear next step.

**Generic problems in PAS.** "You're busy" is generic. "You spend 3 hours a week on email replies that should take 30 minutes" is specific.

**Story without point in SSS.** Every story needs a takeaway the reader can use.

**Mixing frameworks mid-piece.** Pick one and execute it. Don't start AIDA and finish PASTOR.

## Quick Decision Tree

- **Under 100 words?** PAS.
- **Telling a story?** SSS.
- **Selling a transformation?** BAB.
- **Describing a product or feature?** FAB.
- **Long-form sales page?** PASTOR.
- **Mid-length marketing email or landing page?** AIDA.
